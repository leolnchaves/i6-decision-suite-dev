The right order for every POS or store, with the target met by the end of the cycle

Stop repeating last month's order. Smart Order shows what to sell at each point of sale now, including what it does not yet buy but should.

For manufacturers, distributors and retail chains that sell through points of sale.

01The Smart Order

The Smart Order

What to sell, how much and why

For every point of sale, an order built for it: what to sell, how much of each item and the recommended action — add, increase, keep or reduce. Each suggestion comes with its reason, so the sales rep can defend the order and the customer understands why to buy.
  • —One order for each POS, made for its profile
  • —Ready for the sales rep to take on the visit
  • —Basket effort in stars, to prioritize the route
02New items

New items

More space for your brand at the POS

The order separates what the POS already buys from what it doesn't buy yet but fits its profile and that of similar points of sale in the region. That's how the brand gains space: with new items that make sense for that customer, and with their volume tracked against regular items.
  • new items featured
  • new POS gets an order too
03Target within the order

Target within the order

Each customer’s cutoff line

With the cycle target, each customer's order shows what's needed to meet it and what's surplus. The AI also reads each customer's target — loose, balanced, tight or above potential — and explains why, so the manager can act before the cycle ends.
  • —From the easiest to the hardest item to sell
  • —Target reading with the reason
  • —With or without a target, the order comes sorted by effort
04Your levels

Your levels

Orders and targets your way

Every company organizes sales its own way: channel, category, molecule, region, distributor. You choose which columns of your data become levels and in what order, and the whole product follows that hierarchy, from results to each POS order.
  • defined by you
  • applies right from the next cycle
05Measured results

Measured results

What the suggestion changed in the order

Results are measured on your own data: was the suggested item bought more often than the non-suggested one, at the same POS locations? And how did units per order and average ticket compare with similar points of sale? All in plain sight.
  • —Measured on your own data
  • —Results consolidated and per POS
  • —A catalog with prices also reveals the average ticket
06Your data and the cycle

Your data and the cycle

From your data to the next order

You send purchase history, POS portfolio and catalog, by file or integration; the target comes from Forecasting or from your own file. The running cycle is always in view, and you adjust basket size, the turnover or margin objective and the headroom over the target.
  • file or integration
  • short-term cycle

Your next cycle can already start with the right order
